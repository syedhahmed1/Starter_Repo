# Briefing Note: Frontline Attrition at Amazon Fulfillment Centers

**To:** Operations Leadership
**Re:** Attrition risk at the fulfillment center (FC) level
**Date:** July 2026

## Context

Some Amazon FCs have reported turnover north of 100% a year - entire crews effectively turning over more than once in twelve months. Employee reviews from associates and problem-solver-level staff describe HR support as "severely lacking," leave requests as "a nightmare of red tape" when someone is sick or hurt, and a culture where hitting rate matters more than almost anything else, including quality. One review put it bluntly: "constant pressure to maintain exhausting rate without regard for circumstances."

## The problem

This isn't a morale dip that fixes itself. It's a mismatch between how fast FCs need people to move and how little support exists for the people doing the moving. Reviews describe roles that are physically demanding, run through management that's "disorganized and uneven," and missing basic process consistency - things like predictable leave approval and advance notice on workflow changes. None of that shows up on a productivity dashboard, but it shows up in exit interviews and Glassdoor reviews.

## Why it matters now

Stower, Picker, and Packer aren't independent jobs - they're a sequential chain. A mis-stow becomes a failed pick, which becomes a late pack. So losing someone isn't just an open req, it's a weak link that slows the two roles downstream of it. New hires get trained by Learning Ambassadors, who are themselves stretched thin onboarding people faster than usual because turnover keeps creating openings. That's a loop: attrition increases onboarding load, rushed onboarding produces less prepared hires, less prepared hires attrit faster.

## Root causes (working hypothesis)

- Leave and support processes that vary site to site instead of being standardized
- Metrics built around rate, with no real counterweight for quality or sustainability
- Onboarding capacity that hasn't scaled with turnover volume

## Next steps

Before proposing fixes, we'd want two things: exit interview data by site (not just public reviews), and current Learning Ambassador-to-new-hire ratios. That's the minimum needed to tell whether this is a few problem sites or a structural issue across the network.
